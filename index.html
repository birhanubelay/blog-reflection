The Humerous Inside Tragedy: A South African Story

If I were given the chance to give another title to the book "BORN A CRIME", I would have said, "The Humorous Inside Tragedy". This suggests a spark of hope and strength which persists even in the darkest of times. It clearly defines the invincible spirit of Trevor and his mother who found ways to endure the hardships of Apartheid.

Blog Reflection From the Book

"Abel wanted a traditional marriage with a traditional wife...but he never fell in love with subservient women...'He only wants a woman who is free because his dream is to put her in a cage.'"

The Cage Expectations, Freedom, and Control Relationships

Reference image:alamy

The quote deeply describes the exposing paradox of relationships today. It clearly shows a desire for control masked in the name of love. I can briefly explain this in the context of my upbringing as a village girl from the northern part of Ethiopia. Growing up in my village, community expectations of women were for them to perform household chores. Marriages were usually arranged without the consent of the woman and her value was judged by her ability to bear children and by the way she cared for her family especially for her husband. The words "Freedom and Power" were far from existence in the heads of the women in our village. Despite thoseconstraints, some women were defending their individuality, intelligence, and desires. They figured out ways to satisfy their desires and wants.

Abel's desire clearly shows the disturbing truth, that the desire for control and power can disguise itself as love and as longing for one's partner. Trying to break women's spirit, and diminishing them in a cage where men dominated has been a reality for years. When we think of today's world, this phenomenon is happening in relationship dynamics, in the workplace, and in different societal participation, though it has decreased to some extent. The cage is not only a physical space but it also includes the emotional, psychological, and financial situations. As for me, the message I got from this quote is that the struggle for equality and true partnership requires courage to break the shadows of the past and to stand strong in hardships.
